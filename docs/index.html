Abstract

Learning guideline-adherent reasoning from latent decision flows.

Clinical practice guidelines encode evidence-based recommendations as conditional decision flows, which makes them a natural source of process supervision for training language models. Using this supervision is difficult for two reasons. A decision flow consistent with a guideline does not by itself determine the correct answer for a given patient, which we refer to as the path–answer gap, and several valid decision flows can support the same clinical decision, which we refer to as path multiplicity. Under these conditions, answer rewards provide no signal about the decision process, supervised training on annotated flows constrains the model to a single trajectory and reduces answer accuracy, and process rewards computed against one reference flow penalize alternative valid reasoning.

We introduce GUIDER, a joint controller–policy optimization approach that learns guideline-adherent clinical reasoning while accounting for these challenges. The controller learns to generate plausible decision flows from guideline-derived supervision and provides process-level supervision to the policy, while the policy learns to generate clinical reasoning and final decisions. By aggregating process rewards over multiple controller-generated decision flows, GUIDER accounts for path multiplicity, and by gating process rewards on answer correctness, it couples guideline adherence with correct final answers. Joint optimization allows process supervision to evolve with the policy instead of relying on a fixed reference trajectory. The policy is never conditioned on an oracle decision path, so GUIDER requires no access to guidelines at inference time.

On MedGUIDE, GUIDER with Qwen3-14B achieves 82.5 weighted accuracy and 73.5 guideline adherence, improving over the strongest clinical reinforcement learning baseline by 3.3 and 3.1 points, respectively. GUIDER also improves over CRPO, MRPO, and FaithMed on the out-of-domain benchmarks AMEGA-LLM, RealMedQA, and CancerGUIDE with Qwen3-14B and Gemma4-12B backbones.

The challenge

A correct answer does not reveal the path taken to reach it.

01

Path–answer gap

A guideline-consistent decision flow does not necessarily determine the correct patient-specific answer. Process validity and outcome correctness are complementary—not interchangeable.

02

Path multiplicity

Several clinically valid decision flows can support the same decision. Scoring against one fixed reference path can penalize equally sound alternatives.

Distinct clinical decision flows can support the same answer, while no single flow necessarily specifies the complete answer.

The approach

Supervise the process. Preserve the policy’s independence.

GUIDER jointly optimizes a decision-flow controller and a clinical reasoning policy. The controller proposes multiple plausible flows; the policy independently generates its own flow and final answer.

1

Generate

A controller learns from guideline-derived supervision and samples multiple plausible latent decision flows.

2

Aggregate

Score-weighted Monte Carlo aggregation rewards agreement across valid alternatives instead of one oracle trajectory.

3

Gate

Process credit is granted only when the policy’s final answer is correct, bridging process validity and clinical outcome.

4

Optimize jointly

The supervision evolves with the policy, while the policy never observes controller flows or guidelines at inference.

What we learn

Clinical guidelines can provide rich process supervision without becoming an inference-time dependency.

  • Multiple sampled flows capture useful path diversity; gains largely saturate at four flows.
  • Moderately sized decision flows offer enough structure without unnecessary complexity.
  • Improvements transfer across MedGUIDE, AMEGA-LLM, RealMedQA, and CancerGUIDE.
